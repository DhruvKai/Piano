## Overview
Four increasingly complex **left-hand-only** [[Arpeggio]] rhythm patterns. They're all built from the **extended chord**, PIX's name for the root, 5th and octave (C – G – C′). In standard theory that's a [[Power Chord]]. Patterns 1, 3 and 4 add the chord's 3rd back in, up in the higher octave.

> [!info] Octave markings
> The video slides and the PDF colour some notes **red** = "play this an octave higher" (still with the left hand). They're written with a prime mark here — `C′`, `E′`, `G′` — and shown on the upper octave in each diagram.

## Key Concepts
- **Extended chord** = root + 5th + octave, e.g. C G C′, C# G# C#′. The video's rule: *"extended major chord = extended minor chord"*. The 3rd is what makes a chord major or minor, and this chord doesn't have one. See [[Power Chord]].
- **Major vs minor**: Arpeggio 2 only uses the extended chord, so it's the same for C and Cm. Arpeggio 1 (E′) and Arpeggios 3/4 (the C′ E′ G′ triad) use the 3rd. For minor, the 3rd moves down one key: E → D#, F → E, and so on. The tables below give both.
- **All 12 keys**: the video teaches the patterns on C and then shows Arpeggio 1 on C# (`C# G# C#′ G# F′ G# C#′ G#`). Every other root here is the same shape moved to that root.
- `′` after a note = play it one octave higher (the red notes).
- Parentheses `( )` mark notes played together/closer as a group (as shown in the source).
- *"Use the basic 3 notes of any chord to build an arpeggio"*: root, 3rd, 5th of the [[Chord]] are all you need.
- **Fingering (LH):** pinky **5** on the root, index **2** on the 5th, thumb **1** on the octave (`′`). The same numbers work for all 12 roots, black or white.

> [!info] Fingering is a suggestion
> The PDF and video don't print finger numbers. These are the standard choices for an open root–5th–octave LH pattern. Each diagram shows them as orange numbers under the keys.
> - **Root → 5th → octave = 5 – 2 – 1.** This spans a full octave, so keep the hand open and relaxed. If the octave stretch is uncomfortable, use **3** instead of 2 on the 5th.
> - **Arpeggio 1's E′ (the 3rd, an octave up)** is too far to hold the hand still. Keep finger **2** anchored on G, and let the **thumb** move from C′ up to E′ and back.
> - **Arpeggio 3/4's (C′ E′ G′)** is a full triad an octave up, so the **whole hand jumps up** and plays it **5-3-1** (like any [[Chord|triad]]), then jumps back down for (G C′) = 2-1. C′ is therefore thumb in the (G C′) groups but pinky in the triad (shown as `1/5` in the diagrams).
> - On black-key roots the thumb sometimes lands on a black key (e.g. C#′). That's fine here, because the hand is spread open rather than crossing under.

### Arpeggio 3 and 4 — same notes

Arpeggio 3 and 4 use exactly the same notes. Only the time signature changes (4/4 vs 3/4), which moves where the emphasis and counting fall. They share one diagram per root.
