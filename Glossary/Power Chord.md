# Power Chord (PIX's "extended chord")

A **power chord** is just the **root, the 5th and the octave**, with the 3rd left out. On C that's **C – G – C′**. PIX Series calls it an **"extended chord"** in [[Lesson 08 - Advanced Arpeggios]], and every advanced arpeggio there is built on it.

> [!warning] Not the same as [[Extended Chords]]
> In standard theory, "extended chords" are 7ths, 9ths, 11ths and 13ths: *more* notes stacked on a triad. PIX's "extended chord" goes the other way. It drops the 3rd and spreads the [[Chord]] out to the octave. This note covers the PIX meaning.

## How to find it
From the [[Root Note]], count up **7 half steps** (keys, black and white) to the 5th, then go to the next root up for the octave (12 half steps). This is the same 1-5-8 counting as in [[Remembering Chord Notes]], with the 3rd skipped.

## Why major = minor
The only difference between a major and a minor [[Chord]] is the 3rd. A power chord has no 3rd, so **C major and C minor have the same power chord** (C – G – C′). The video puts it as *"extended major chord = extended minor chord"*, and shows C#m as C# – G# – C#′, the same as C#.

An arpeggio pattern built only from these three notes (like Lesson 08's Arpeggio 2) works under major and minor songs alike. Once a pattern adds the 3rd back in (Arpeggio 1, 3 and 4), you need the major or the minor version.

## All 12 keys

| Root | Power chord | Root | Power chord |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | C – G – C′ | F# | F# – C# – F#′ |
| C# | C# – G# – C#′ | G | G – D – G′ |
| D | D – A – D′ | G# | G# – D# – G#′ |
| D# | D# – A# – D#′ | A | A – E – A′ |
| E | E – B – E′ | A# | A# – F – A#′ |
| F | F – C – F′ | B | B – F# – B′ |

Every one of these is drawn on a keyboard as Arpeggio 2 in [[Lesson 08 - Advanced Arpeggios#Keyboard diagrams by root|Lesson 08's diagrams]].

> [!tip] Fingering (LH)
> Pinky **5** on the root, index **2** on the 5th, thumb **1** on the octave. The same shape works on every root, black or white.
